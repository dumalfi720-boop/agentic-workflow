Module 3.1 • ~45 min

🔎 Diagnosis of real bottlenecks

Learn to see process, waste, priority and real opportunity before proposing agents.

What is it:

describe steps, inputs, outputs, actors and systems involved in the current process.

Why learn:

Without a process map, the proposal becomes a guess and not a diagnosis.

Key concepts:

as-is, actor, handoff and step visibility.

What is it:

locate rework, waiting, low standardization and recurring errors.

Why learn:

Commercial value arises from the real bottleneck, not from the fascination with technology.

Key concepts:

waste, rework, delay and inconsistency.

What is it:

filter opportunities by frequency, value, feasibility and risk.

Why learn:

Not every bottleneck should become an agentic project.

Key concepts:

fit, feasibility, impact and scope.

What is it:

identify scenarios where fixed automation, spreadsheet or process adjustment work best.

Why learn:

Good consultancy also eliminates bad proposals.

Key concepts:

overengineering, simpler fix, process redesign and no-go.

What is it:

evaluate impact, dependence, criticality and chance of adoption.

Why learn:

Poorly defined priority consumes energy on the wrong project.

Key concepts:

priority matrix, risk level, dependency and quick win.

What is it:

generate a simple diagnosis with bottlenecks, priority and eligible case.

Why learn:

This document opens the proposal, scope and commercial conversation.

Key concepts:

discovery, opportunity map, priority and fit.

Module 3.2 • ~45 min

🧭 Use cases by area

Translate agentic into applications by business function, without becoming a generic list of ideas.

What is it:

explore use in research, briefing, assisted production and campaign analysis.

Why learn:

Marketing is usually the entry point for agentic projects.

Key concepts:

briefing, content ops, analysis and workflow.

What is it:

cases for enrichment, qualification, personalization and meeting preparation.

Why learn:

sales show quick returns when well trimmed.

Key concepts:

lead scoring, enrichment, follow-up and preparation.

What is it:

use in classification, prioritization, assisted response and human handoff.

Why learn:

service requires clear governance and therefore teaches the limits of agentic well.

Key concepts:

triage, priority, escalation and SLA.

What is it:

cases linked to backoffice, reconciliation, processing and operational monitoring.

Why learn:

operations concentrates high-return silent bottlenecks.

Key concepts:

ops workflow, exception handling and routine automation.

What is it:

evaluate scenarios in onboarding, documents, analysis and internal communication.

Why learn:

These areas require more risk criteria and approval.

Key concepts:

sensitive data, approval, compliance and policy.

What is it:

organize cases by area, priority and risk.

Why learn:

This transforms repertoire into sales and discovery material.

Key concepts:

use case backlog, sector fit and priority stack.

Module 3.3 • ~45 min

📑 Technical-commercial scope

Translate opportunity into a clear, limited and defensible proposal.

What is it:

compare the current process with the desired operation after the intervention.

Why learn:

This comparison anchors the proposal in concrete change.

Key concepts:

current state, target state and delta.

What is it:

record access, data availability, team, deadlines and dependencies.

Why learn:

hidden premise becomes conflict later.

Key concepts:

assumption, dependency, access and readiness.

What is it:

list technical, operational, adoption and data risk.

Why learn:

A mature proposal shows risk and does not hide it.

Key concepts:

risk register, mitigation and dependency risk.

What is it:

delimit first delivery by module, integration and result.

Why learn:

Small scope closes more contracts and generates less friction.

Key concepts:

MVP, phased scope, milestone and acceptance.

What is it:

break the project into phases, responsible parties and validation milestones.

Why learn:

Without a timeline, the proposal seems abstract and risky.

Key concepts:

timeline, dependency, milestone and handoff.

What is it:

put together a short proposal with scope, assumptions, risk and plan.

Why learn:

This is the bridge between discovery and commercial closure.

Key concepts:

proposal, scope doc, assumptions and timeline.

Module 3.4 • ~45 min

💰 ROI and pricing

Calculate value, support price and avoid sales based solely on hours.

What is it:

measure hours saved per execution, week and month.

Why learn:

time is still a simple and strong way of translating value.

Key concepts:

time saved, volume, frequency and baseline.

What is it:

translate error reduction into avoided cost, better SLA or lower loss.

Why learn:

Many projects pay more for errors avoided than for time saved.

Key concepts:

error rate, rework cost and quality gain.

What is it:

calculate what the company loses by correcting the bottleneck.

Why learn:

Perceived value increases when the proposal shows avoided financial risk.

Key concepts:

cost avoidance, delay cost and leakage.

What is it:

estimate revenue captured by better speed, coverage or conversion.

Why learn:

projects linked to growth tend to have a higher pricing ceiling.

Key concepts:

revenue lift, conversion and response speed.

What is it:

price in relation to the value delivered, risk assumed and defined scope.

Why learn:

Those who master commercial value stop selling just technical effort.

Key concepts:

value pricing, anchor and outcome pricing.

What is it:

transform time, error and revenue into a lean economic argument.

Why learn:

This speeds up trading and reduces price objections.

Key concepts:

ROI sheet, baseline, earnings and payback.

Module 3.5 • ~45 min

🚚 Delivery and adoption

Closing is not enough: you need to onboard, validate acceptance and make the customer use it.

What is it:

align those responsible, access, initial goal and way of working.

Why learn:

Bad deployment often starts with lax onboarding.

Key concepts:

kickoff, roles, access and expected outcome.

What is it:

secure necessary accounts, permissions, environments, and secrets.

Why learn:

Much of the delay is a real, operational, non-technical project.

Key concepts:

credentials, access path, environment and owner.

What is it:

define acceptance criteria, guided testing and validation evidence.

Why learn:

Without clear acceptance, delivery remains eternally open.

Key concepts:

acceptance criteria, UAT and sign-off.

What is it:

plan entry into production by phase, group or volume.

Why learn:

Gradual rollout reduces risk and accelerates customer confidence.

Key concepts:

pilot, staged rollout, feedback loop and expansion.

What is it:

establish support window, priority, response time and improvement routine.

Why learn:

Project without clear maintenance degrades commercial relationship.

Key concepts:

SLA, support model, maintenance and evolution.

What is it:

document onboarding, acceptance, rollout and initial support.

Why learn:

This converts on-time delivery into a long-term relationship.

Key concepts:

handoff plan, rollout, support and adoption.

Module 3.6 • ~45 min

🧰 Portfolio, offer and product

Packaging know-how into a case, clear offer and possibility of recurrence.

What is it:

tell the problem, solution, result and learning in a salesable way.

Why learn:

case reduces the need to convince from scratch.

Key concepts:

case study, before-after, proof and outcome.

What is it:

organize service in name, scope, result and price range.

Why learn:

Clear offer sells better than vague “on demand” service.

Key concepts:

offer, package, deliverable and scope line.

What is it:

think about monitoring, maintenance, new phases and support product.

Why learn:

Recurrence stabilizes business and deepens customer relationships.

Key concepts:

retainer, maintenance plan, roadmap and upsell.

What is it:

define niche, type of case and value language of your work.

Why learn:

The market chooses more easily who communicates focus.

Key concepts:

niche, positioning, ideal client and category fit.

What is it:

evolve from task executor to results and evolution partner.

Why learn:

This increases margin, predictability and influences the account.

Key concepts:

strategic partner, retainer and trusted advisor.

What is it:

put together a lean offer with problem, result and next step.

Why learn:

This becomes a reusable commercial asset.

Key concepts:

offer page, case page, CTA and positioning proof.

Module 3.7 • ~50 min

🏁 Business Capstone

End the course with a complete proposal for a real or simulated company.

What is it:

apply discovery to a complete business case.

Why learn:

the capstone needs to show discretion from the beginning.

Key concepts:

diagnosis, pain point and opportunity.

What is it:

specify the proposed flow, integrations and delivery method.

Why learn:

The solution needs to appear implementable, not conceptual.

Key concepts:

solution design, scope and implementation path.

What is it:

attach baseline, expected gain and return range.

Why learn:

strong capstone needs to answer “why is this valid now?”.

Key concepts:

ROI, payback and outcome estimate.

What is it:

Break execution into phases, acceptance and dependencies.

Why learn:

pitch without a concrete plan is unlikely to close.

Key concepts:

execution plan, milestone and rollout.

What is it:

explain the problem, proposal, value and next step in decision-maker language.

Why learn:

good solutions also need to be sold well.

Key concepts:

pitch, objection handling, clarity and CTA.

What is it:

present diagnosis, solution, ROI and plan for final evaluation.

Why learn:

ends the course with sellable and defended delivery.

Key concepts:

capstone, proposal defense, business case and next step.

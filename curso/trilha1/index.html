Module 1.1 • ~35 min

🧭 What is Agentic AI and how to think like an engineer

Opening module focused on criteria, scope and work breakdown.

What is it:

operational comparison between punctual response, fixed flow and goal-oriented execution.

Why learn:

avoid selling or building agentic for problems that call for deterministic automation.

Key concepts:

objective, limited autonomy, scope and predictability.

What is it:

definition of the work, available information and execution limits.

Why learn:

Without this, the agent improvises and quality drops.

Key concepts:

input, output, restrictions and acceptance criteria.

What is it:

objective definition of what is a correct, incomplete or dangerous exit.

Why learn:

Without objective criteria there is no consistent improvement.

Key concepts:

correctness, completeness, format and security.

What is it:

separating a larger objective into verifiable substeps.

Why learn:

Good workflows are born from good decomposition, not from bigger prompts.

Key concepts:

phases, checkpoints, handoffs and partial validation.

What is it:

architectural criterion for separating deterministic parts and agentic parts.

Why learn:

Many errors arise from poorly distributed autonomy.

Key concepts:

risk, cost, branching and guardrails.

What is it:

exercise to transform a need into an operational spec.

Why learn:

filters curiosity and initiates agentic engineering practice.

Key concepts:

spec, contract, expected output and acceptable risk.

Module 1.2 • ~40 min

🔌 Technical fundamentals for workflows

Minimum technical reading to integrate systems, contracts and tools with basic security.

What is it:

communication between systems using endpoint, method, payload and response.

Why learn:

Without understanding the API, you cannot design a reliable tool.

Key concepts:

GET, POST, headers, status code and timeout.

What is it:

data format for moving information between steps.

Why learn:

Poorly structured output breaks integrations.

Key concepts:

schema, mandatory fields, parser and serialization.

What is it:

mechanism that triggers executions when an event happens.

Why learn:

Real flows are usually born by event, not by manual prompt.

Key concepts:

trigger, signature, queue and idempotence.

What is it:

Protection of credentials outside the code and the prompt.

Why learn:

agents without segregating secrets becomes an operational risk.

Key concepts:

token, scope, rotation and least privilege.

What is it:

agreement of accepted inputs, outputs and error states.

Why learn:

Without a contract, the agent confuses a technical and business error.

Key concepts:

fallback, retry, recoverable error and validation.

What is it:

exercise to consume endpoint, validate response and generate usable output.

Why learn:

It makes the student get out of theory and deal with real integration.

Key concepts:

request, parse, error and standardized output.

Module 1.3 • ~35 min

🧱 WAT in practice

Separate workflow, agents and tools to avoid confusing and poorly auditable systems.

What is it:

layer that defines phases, transitions and local validations.

Why learn:

Without an explicit workflow, debugging gets much worse.

Key concepts:

phases, transition, checkpoint and gate.

What is it:

layer that interprets objective and chooses the next action.

Why learn:

It helps to provide autonomy only where there is value.

Key concepts:

planning, choice of tool and context.

What is it:

specialized functions for reading, writing, querying or transforming data.

Why learn:

Well-designed tools reduce model improvisation.

Key concepts:

single responsibility, schema and side effect.

What is it:

controlled transition between agents, subtasks or phases.

Why learn:

Clear composition allows it to grow without becoming monolithic prompt.

Key concepts:

handoff, payload and ownership.

What is it:

recording of steps, decisions, inputs, outputs and errors.

Why learn:

Without visibility there is no proof of value or good debugging.

Key concepts:

trace, log and correlation id.

What is it:

exercise to design workflow, agentic decisions and necessary tools.

Why learn:

first step to moving away from loose pieces and arriving at coherent architecture.

Key concepts:

map, border, responsibility and dependence.

Module 1.4 • ~45 min

🧰 Tool design and schemas

Design small, predictable and safe tools to increase agent quality.

What is it:

text that explains what the tool is for, when to use it and when not to use it.

Why learn:

Bad description leads to wrong calls even with good implementation.

Key concepts:

intent, scope and misuse.

What is it:

definition of expected fields and response format.

Why learn:

Clear schema reduces errors and facilitates validation.

Key concepts:

required, optional, enum and output shape.

What is it:

checking type, format and consistency before side effect.

Why learn:

prevents bad input from turning into wrong action.

Key concepts:

parse, coercion and precondition.

What is it:

Classification of failures in cases of retry, fallback or interruption.

Why learn:

helps contain cost and bad loops.

Key concepts:

retry, backoff and terminal error.

What is it:

manual checkpoint before sending, deleting or changing sensitive state.

Why learn:

Agentic engineering fails when there is too much autonomy in risky operations.

Key concepts:

approval gate, preview and human-in-the-loop.

What is it:

create tools with schema, test invalid inputs and adjust descriptions.

Why learn:

shows that the quality of the tools defines the quality of the agent.

Key concepts:

schema, validation, misuse and guardrail.

Module 1.5 • ~40 min

🧠 Context, memory and reuse

Control what the system knows, what it needs to remember and what should be reused.

What is it:

immediate and relevant information for current execution.

Why learn:

too much context increases cost and noise; Too little reduces quality.

Key concepts:

window, relevance and pruning.

What is it:

data saved to reuse in future runs.

Why learn:

without persistent memory the agent repeats questions and loses state.

Key concepts:

history, state, cache and profile.

What is it:

record of execution progress with clear pause and resume milestones.

Why learn:

checkpoints reduce failure costs and facilitate partial reprocessing.

Key concepts:

resume, checkpoint and partial output.

What is it:

instruction packages and patterns that reduce repetition and increase consistency.

Why learn:

without reuse, the agent loses pattern between projects.

Key concepts:

instruction pack, defaults and configuration.

What is it:

practice of filtering, summarizing and prioritizing information before sending it to the agent.

Why learn:

Dirty context makes decisions worse and execution more expensive.

Key concepts:

relevance, summarization and stale context.

What is it:

exercise to save minimal history and resume a flow.

Why learn:

shows how context and memory change reliability and experience.

Key concepts:

save state, restore and replay.

Module 1.6 • ~50 min

🚀 First agentic mini project

Closing the trail with a guided research and synthesis project oriented to structured output.

What is it:

choosing a case with a clear objective, available inputs and useful output.

Why learn:

A poorly chosen case mixes technical difficulty with an ill-defined problem.

Key concepts:

scope, input, output and value.

What is it:

design of phases with decision points and intermediate outputs.

Why learn:

Good specification reduces improvisation and makes the project testable.

Key concepts:

spec, phases, gate and deliverable.

What is it:

definition of which tools are included and when each one will be used.

Why learn:

Good design uses few well-fitted tools.

Key concepts:

tool chain, dependency and schema.

What is it:

checking the final output and intermediate steps against defined criteria.

Why learn:

It trains the discipline of not confusing execution with quality.

Key concepts:

acceptance criteria, format check and completeness.

What is it:

organization of output in a readable, standardized and reusable format.

Why learn:

good engineering ends in usable delivery, not just correct execution.

Key concepts:

report, structured output and handoff.

What is it:

short presentation of the flow, results and technical lessons.

Why learn:

retro consolidates criteria and prepares the next iteration.

Key concepts:

demo, postmortem, iteration and next step.
